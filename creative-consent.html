What Happens to Your Work When You Post It

Every platform has already decided whether your content can be used to train AI. Most of them decided it in a document you agreed to without reading. This is that decision, platform by platform, in plain language.

If you post a video on one platform, it may be training a model tomorrow. Post the same video somewhere else and the platform's own terms forbid that. Nothing on the surface tells you which is which, and the answer is buried in a terms-of-service document that changes without telling you.

Creative Consent badges fix that. Three ratings, one per platform, describing exactly one thing: what the platform's own terms permit when it comes to training AI on the work you post there. We read the terms, quote the clause that decides it, link the source, and date it.

This is not a ranking of which platforms are good. It is a statement of what you are agreeing to. Some creators will read a No Protection rating and post anyway, because the reach is worth it. That is a legitimate choice. It should just be an informed one.

The three badges

CCF Full Protection

The platform's terms prohibit using your content to train AI. It does not train its own generative models on your work, and it does not share your work with third parties for training. This is the strongest position a platform can take, and very few take it.

CCO Optional Protection

Your content can be used for AI training, but you can stop it. There is a clear, free opt-out. In most cases you are opted in by default, so the protection only exists if you go and switch it on. Opt-outs are also usually forward-looking: they stop future use and do not remove work already absorbed into a model.

CCN No Protection

The platform's terms permit training on your content and there is no way to opt out. Usually this rides on a broad, perpetual, royalty-free license you granted when you signed up. Posting is the consent.

Under review

Some platforms do not yet have a rating here, and we would rather say so than guess. These are cases where a platform's stated policy and its practical effect point in different directions, or where the control that exists covers less than it appears to. We show you the clause and explain the open question. A withheld rating is not a low rating, and it is not an accusation. It means we have not finished the work.

Full Protection

CCF

Vimeo

Verified 2026-08-11

Vimeo's AI Addendum states it will not allow generative AI models to be trained on videos hosted on the platform without explicit user consent, including on free accounts. It also prohibits unauthorized scraping by model companies, and its third-party providers have agreed not to train on user inputs or outputs.

What to keep in mind

A policy against scraping is not a technical barrier. Vimeo commits not to permit training and to pursue unauthorized scraping, which is meaningfully more than most platforms offer, but public video remains reachable by a determined scraper.

Source: Vimeo Artificial Intelligence Addendum (dated 3 March 2026) and Vimeo's position on AI. Retrieved 2026-08-11.

Ghost

Needs date stamp

Ghost's terms do not claim any right to use your content for AI training. The license you grant is broad in form but explicitly bounded in purpose.

The clause that decides it

"perpetual, irrevocable, world-wide, sublicensable, royalty-free, and non-exclusive" license, limited to "displaying, distributing and promoting your site"

What to keep in mind

Ghost's terms are silent on AI training rather than prohibitive. Silence means they claim no right to do it, which is why this rates Full Protection, but it also means you may want robots.txt rules of your own against outside crawlers. Ghost is self-hosted or Ghost-hosted, so that control is genuinely yours.

Source: Ghost Terms of Service. Retrieval date to confirm.

Medium

Needs date stamp

Medium has stated a default opt-out from AI training and updated its robots.txt to block AI crawlers. Protection is on by default rather than something you have to find.

What to keep in mind

"a nonexclusive, royalty-free, worldwide, fully paid, and sublicensable license to use, reproduce, modify, adapt, publish, translate, create derivative works from, distribute, publicly perform and display your content… in all media formats and distribution methods now known or later developed"

The general content license remains broad. It is not an AI-training license and Medium's stated position is against training, but the breadth is worth knowing about.

Source: Medium Terms of Service. Retrieval date to confirm.

Needs date stamp

LinkedIn uses member data for AI training and provides an opt-out in settings.

The clause that decides it

YouTube

Tier withheld

YouTube provides a setting in Studio Content Manager controlling whether third-party companies may train AI models on your videos.

Why we are not rating it yet

That setting governs third parties. Google's own use of YouTube content to train Google's own models is a separate question, and the third-party control does not clearly reach it. Rating YouTube as Optional Protection on the strength of a control that may not cover the largest AI developer with access to the content would tell you something misleading. We are establishing the scope of Google's own use before we assign a tier.

What to keep in mind now

The main Terms of Service do not discuss AI training at all. Everything on this subject lives in separate policy pages and Studio settings, which is itself worth knowing.

Source: YouTube Terms of Service plus Studio Content Manager settings. Needs verification and a date.

Meta: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Threads

Tier withheld

Meta uses content and interactions to train its AI and offers an objection mechanism.

Why we are not rating it yet

How real that objection mechanism is depends heavily on where you live. It is substantially more available to users covered by GDPR in the EU and UK than to users in the United States. A single global rating would be wrong for one of those audiences, so we are deciding whether to rate to the weakest jurisdiction or to show the split.

The clause

"Meta may share certain information about you that is processed when using the AIs with third parties… By using AIs, you are instructing us to share your information with third parties… Those third parties will use any information we share – which may include personal information about you or others – in accordance with those third parties' privacy policies"

What to keep in mind now

Meta also states it may review your interactions with its AI features, including message content, and that review "may be automated or manual (human)."

Source: Meta AI Terms of Service. Retrieval date to confirm.

BlueSky

Tier withheld

BlueSky has publicly said it does not intend to train generative AI on user data. It is also built on an open API, so public posts are trivially collectable by third parties.

Why we are not rating it yet

This is a question about our own method rather than about BlueSky. If the badge measures what a platform's terms permit, BlueSky rates well. If it measures real-world exposure, it does not. We cannot rate BlueSky low for scraping it does not control while rating other platforms with comparable stated policies high, so we are settling the method first. Our working answer is that the badge should measure platform policy, with scraping exposure shown separately on every card.

Source: BlueSky Terms of Service. Retrieval date to confirm.

How we rate, and what these badges do not tell you

We read the platform's own published terms, privacy policy, and AI-specific policies. We quote the clause that decides the rating, link the source, and record the date we read it. Where a platform's stated position and its practical effect differ, we say so on the card rather than burying it in the rating.

These badges describe permission, not behavior. A Full Protection rating means the platform's terms forbid training on your work. It is not a finding that no model has ever been trained on it. Scrapers ignore terms, and a rating cannot stop them.

Opt-outs are almost never retroactive. On most Optional Protection platforms, switching the setting off stops future use and leaves anything already absorbed exactly where it is.

Terms change without notice. Several platforms on this page changed their AI position in the last two years, sometimes before updating the terms that described it. Every card carries the date we last checked. If a date looks old, treat it as old.

We rate the platform, not the company's whole conduct. This is a narrow question about one thing: what the terms permit regarding AI training on work you post.

If you are a platform and we have this wrong

We would rather be accurate than fast. If your terms have changed, if we have read a clause wrongly, or if a control exists that we did not find, tell us and we will correct the entry and note the correction date on the card.
