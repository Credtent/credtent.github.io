Guide

How AI training data licensing works.

AI training data licensing is a written agreement in which the owner of text, images, audio, video, or data grants permission to use specific works to train a model, on defined terms.

This guide answers the questions rights holders ask us about licensing their work for AI training.

Updated November 2026. Published by Credtent, a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation.

What is AI training data licensing?

It is a written agreement in which the owner of text, images, audio, video, or data grants an AI developer permission to use specific works to train a model, in exchange for payment and on defined terms.

A license names:

  • the works
  • the permitted uses
  • the duration
  • the documentation the developer receives

Each side has its own question:

  • The developer wants to know that the people granting permission had the right to grant it.
  • The rights holder wants to know what the work will be used for, who else can use it, and how payment reaches the people who made it.

In the European Union

In the European Union, the text and data mining exception in Article 4 of Directive (EU) 2019/790 applies only where rights holders have not expressly reserved their rights, and for content published online that reservation can be made by machine-readable means. Once a rights holder has reserved those rights, a developer who still wants the work needs permission, and a license is how permission is given.

What rights does a training license grant?

A training license lets a developer use named works to train or fine-tune a model, within the scope the contract states.

It does not automatically cover:

  • inference or grounding, where content is retrieved to answer a question
  • a transfer of copyright
  • any indemnity

Some programs license grounding separately. Microsoft's Publisher Content Marketplace, announced on February 3, 2026, is built for grounding.

A license conveys only the rights its signers hold, so a careful license comes with a manifest of the works covered and a record of who warranted which rights.

Licenses include the rights the contracting holders warranted, a named manifest, and a disclosure pack your counsel can use for EU AI Act Art. 53 and CA AB 2013 filings. Those statutes still bind the developer. Indemnity, if any, is only as written in the signed license.

Who can offer a license?

Licenses come from four kinds of sellers:

  • Individual creatives, directly or through an agent, a marketplace, or a collective program.
  • Publishers, studios, labels, archives, and data providers that own catalogs.
  • Aggregators and marketplaces, which resell licenses or act as an agent and route payment back to owners.
  • Collective programs that offer one set of terms to many participants, such as Copyright Clearance Center's AI Systems Training License.

How are licenses priced?

Four structures are common:

  • a flat fee for a defined collection
  • a per-work or per-unit rate
  • a revenue share between seller and rights holders
  • usage-based fees tied to how often content is accessed

The price depends on exclusivity, quality, documentation, the rights layers involved, and the license term.

Credtent does not publish a rate card for AI companies.

Under its standard terms, Credtent routes 85% of each licensing deal to the rights holders and data providers whose content was licensed and keeps 15% for the platform, legal work, compliance tools, and operations. The Terms of Service list the costs and taxes deducted before payment.

Usage-based pricing appears in access and grounding programs. Cloudflare's pay per crawl, which its own page describes as a private beta feature of AI Crawl Control, lets publishers charge AI crawlers for access.

What records do developers need?

  • European Union: Developers of general-purpose AI models in the EU must publish a sufficiently detailed summary of training content and keep a copyright policy that honors rights reservations, under Article 53 of the EU AI Act. Under Article 113, those obligations apply from 2 August 2025.
  • California: California's AB 2013 (Chapter 817, approved September 28, 2024) requires developers to post training-data documentation, including the sources or owners of the datasets and whether they were purchased or licensed.

A developer's filings remain the developer's responsibility. A good license gives the people who write them a manifest of named works, a record of who warranted which rights, and delivery dates.

Can a license cover content a model already trained on?

A deal can address past training only for the rights holders who sign it.

We can structure a going-forward license and a covenant not to sue over past use from the rights holders who sign, limited to the named works. Neither one binds a rights holder who did not sign, and neither one satisfies the developer's regulatory obligations.

Rights holders who did not sign keep every claim they had.

Frequently asked questions

Does a training license include answers and excerpts?

Not automatically. Inference, grounding, and display need their own written terms.

Can a license fix past training for everyone?

No. We can structure a going-forward license and a covenant not to sue over past use from the rights holders who sign, limited to the named works. Neither one binds a rights holder who did not sign, and neither one satisfies the developer's regulatory obligations.
