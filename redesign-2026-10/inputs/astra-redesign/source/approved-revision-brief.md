### Valuation as a prominent standalone service

Valuation is an important initial reason customers find Credtent, including customers who already have a buyer or a licensing agreement. It must be prominently presented near the audience introductions, with a **Request a valuation** action and a dedicated explanation/page.

**Understand your content’s value. Explore your licensing options.**

Suggested page opening discussed: **What is your content worth for AI licensing?**

Cover three situations:

1. Before a first agreement.
2. Reassessing value after an initial deal.
3. Exploring additional licensing opportunities.

Explain that Credtent provides a baseline valuation when content is added, with optional **paid custom valuation** for deeper assessment. Do not invent custom-assessment prices, delivery times, or a claim that baseline valuation is free unless separately confirmed.

The assessment considers intended AI use alongside creator-side evidence: cultural significance, audience reception, awards, and other supported indicators of value. Do not reduce the explanation to volume or tokens. Ratings and other third-party evidence require appropriate sourcing. Explain the methodology and limitations without presenting an estimate as a guaranteed transaction price or an established industry standard.

Customers may obtain valuation independently, then choose whether to seek additional help finding licensing partners. Existing customer anecdotes are internal context, not approved public case studies. Do not publish identifying details, deal values, or unsupported outcome claims.

Eric expressly wants public copy focused on constructive pricing, informed agreements, and partnerships. Do not market valuation as litigation support or introduce lawsuits into these sections.

### Research foundation

Model-collapse discussion belongs in a sourced explanation with appropriate limits:

- [Shumailov et al., Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) examines degradation from recursively training on generated data under specified conditions.
- [Gerstgrasser et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413) found that retaining original real data while accumulating synthetic data avoided collapse in their experiments.

These support discussing data origins, diversity, and careful selection. They do not establish that all synthetic data is harmful, human authorship alone guarantees quality, or purchasing a license prevents model collapse. Publish only supported claims and separate research findings from Credtent’s proposed value.
