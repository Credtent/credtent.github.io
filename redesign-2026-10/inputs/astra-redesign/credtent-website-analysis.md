## Recommendation

**Founder-confirmed mission and tone:** Credtent supports AI innovation and adoption as a neutral third-party utility serving both AI companies and individual creatives. It curates trustworthy information to help people navigate rapid change, exercise informed choice, and participate in the AI economy. Both sites should convey constructive possibility, respect for human creativity, and practical support for responsible AI development. Explain benefits for each participant, recognize improvements, and reduce information overload through clear summaries with accessible evidence. Creator protection, voluntary licensing, and responsible AI use belong to the same mission. Preserve a creator's ability to opt out without pressure to license.

**Positioning refinement from the founder:** use **AI realism**, superseding the earlier suggestion of an optimistic tone. The ambition is to raise the floor as well as the ceiling: make AI's benefits available to more people while supporting consent, compensation, human agency, and responsible commercial adoption. Credtent connects participants and facilitates partnerships. The founder's phrase **collaborithmic partnership** describes collaboration with algorithms that expands human capability while retaining human judgment and avoiding cognitive surrender. Present this as the founder's philosophy; do not portray it as an externally validated technical standard.

Editorial recommendation: use plain-language descriptions of these benefits on conversion pages and introduce the distinctive founder terminology with an explanation on the About page or a supporting essay. Include addressing past-use concerns within the commercial licensing discussion, subject to verified service capability and the scope of particular agreements. Describe reduced risk and negotiated resolution as objectives, without promising elimination of lawsuits, universal releases, or retroactive coverage beyond what relevant parties agree.

**Founder-confirmed editorial requirement:** Credtent will not publish factual claims without credible, citable support. Trust is a central brand requirement. AI-assisted terms-of-service analysis should make complex policies understandable and help creators identify concerns. The underlying source remains the basis for the claim; generated analysis must be checked against it. Clearly distinguish direct policy statements, Credtent's interpretation, and unresolved questions. Do not infer a company's motives or actual training practices from ambiguous terms.

Recommended implementation of that requirement: present a short plain-language finding, a link to the supporting clause or policy, the reviewed version/date and applicable scope, and available creator actions. Keep deeper evidence expandable so the source is accessible without overwhelming the reader. Use a documented review and correction process for published assessments, including human review of consequential interpretations. This is a proposed editorial workflow, not a claim about an existing operational process. Describe the resource as educational analysis rather than individualized legal advice. Unverified claims remain unpublished or are explicitly identified as unresolved where that uncertainty itself is useful.

Proceed with the two-site strategy. Credtent.org should help individual creators exercise choice, understand platforms, and follow what happens to their work. Credtent.com should help content owners and AI companies establish and manage licensing relationships.

The supplied master brief is a useful foundation, but I would revise it before implementation. Its main omissions are the creator auditing journey, citation licensing, and practical education about participating in the AI economy. It also conflates the subjects of the badge families and contains technical recommendations that need correction.

The shared message should be constructive: creators have agency, responsible AI businesses can obtain permission, and both can benefit from a functioning licensing economy. Opting out must remain a complete, respected choice, without pressure to license later.

## Migration and implementation approach

Two public sites do not inherently require two unrelated codebases. Review the existing implementation first, preserve useful work, and choose shared components with independently deployable surfaces if feasible. Hosting remains an implementation decision until the existing projects and redirect capabilities are inspected.

The migration is partial: commercial pages move, while the creator resource property remains on .org. Keep that distinction throughout the URL plan. Do not redirect the entire .org property to .com.

The brief's suggestion to redirect the enterprise portion of a pricing page is not a complete HTTP routing rule. A page section cannot independently receive an ordinary server-side redirect. Choose a destination for the old URL as a whole, or preserve a short routing page that serves both existing audiences. Review fragment links and inbound traffic before making that choice.

Prepare an inventory of old pages, aliases, important inbound destinations, and the final URL for each. Avoid redirecting unrelated pages to a homepage. Use direct permanent redirects for genuinely moved pages and update internal links. Google recommends retaining migration redirects generally for at least a year. [Site-migration guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)

Recommended sequence:

1. Resolve product scope and definitions, confirm the brand direction, and inspect the existing sites and integrations.
2. Produce both homepage designs, navigation, representative detail pages, and proposed copy in preview.
3. Build and verify the primary opt-out and commercial contact journeys, reusing existing working flows where practical.
4. Add the essential resources, badge explanations, metadata, and approved measurement behavior. Account access can be pursued alongside design work; it need not block drafts.
5. Review both sites together, test the URL map and real completion paths, and obtain the requested launch decision after the revisions discussion.
6. Cut over with the previous deployment and routing configuration retained for recovery. Verify both sites, forms, registration links, canonical URLs, sitemaps, and permitted analytics behavior after release.

A production cutover deserves a recovery plan. It should not be described as an inherently irreversible step.
